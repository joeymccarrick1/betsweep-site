    The big number on a tile is the player's fantasy points a game over his last 3 games (his last 2 if he has played only 4 or 5); the arrow is the change from his earlier games. To make a Rising list a player has to average at least 12 fantasy points as a quarterback, 8 as a back or receiver, or 6 as a tight end over those games, be up at least 1 a game, and have beaten his earlier average in at least two of them, so one big game is not a rise. A Falling list is the mirror: he averaged that much before, is down at least 1 a game, and was under his old average in at least two of those games. Players ruled out or doubtful this week, and players who missed their team's last two games, are left off the lists; the search still finds them. Each card at the top names the leader in one count, among players who could make a list. Breakout is the biggest riser whose earlier average was under the mark a riser has to reach. Season high is the highest score that was a player's best of the season and came in his latest game. On the climb is the most games in a row with more points than the game before. Highest floor is the best worst game among regular starters (six or more games). A top-12 week is a week he finished among the 12 highest scorers at his position, counting every player; a week still being played is not counted. In a player's box, Rank now is where his last-3 average stands at his position among players who played one of their team's last two games; the season rank is by points a game among players with four or more games. Both count the players on this page. A rate such as yards per carry is total over total for the games shown; games with too few chances to mean anything are left out. What moved is arithmetic on his latest games and his earlier ones (a rushing or receiving touchdown is 6 points, a passing touchdown 4). This week's projection is BetSweep's model in full PPR, the number Start / Sit shows; a defense ranked 1st in yards allowed gives up the most.
